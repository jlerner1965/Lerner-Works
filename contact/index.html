What happens after you get in touch

Before we speak, I look at your current site and your Google listing on a phone, the way a customer would, so the call starts with something concrete.

On the call, you tell me what's broken and what a good month looks like. I'll tell you what I noticed, and whether I think a website is even the problem. Twenty minutes, sometimes less.

Within two business days, if it makes sense to continue, you get a short written diagnosis and a one-page scope with a fixed price and a date. Nothing starts until you've read it and said yes.

If it doesn't make sense, I'll say so and, where I can, point you to someone better placed. No follow-up sequence, no chasing.

Prefer to write?

Send a brief instead. Five lines is enough.

Some people would rather set the business down in writing than talk it through. The button opens an email to me with the five prompts on the right already in it; answer the ones you can and leave the rest. I reply within one business day, with either a few questions or a time to talk.

You don't need a plan, a budget or a list of pages. What helps most is the one thing you wish the website did that it doesn't do now.

Email a brief

What to put in it

  1. What the business does, and whereTwo sentences. The town matters more than the industry.
  2. The current website, if there is oneA link is plenty. If there isn't one, say what you send people to instead.
  3. The one thing you wish it didMore calls, fewer wrong inquiries, bookings without the phone tag. Whatever it is.
  4. When you'd like it liveA month is comfortable; two to three weeks is the usual build. Say if there's a date behind it.
  5. Which tier looks closestStarter, Growth, Care, or "not sure". The prices are posted, so you can say before we speak.
