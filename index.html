How I work

Four things you can hold me to.

01

Diagnosis before design

The first thing I produce is a written account of what is actually costing you customers, with evidence. Sometimes the answer is not a new website, and I'll tell you that on the first call.

02

Specific beats polished

Real service names, real numbers, real photographs of real jobs. A page that says exactly what you do and for whom converts better than one that merely looks expensive.

03

Fast, plain and yours

Hand-written pages that load quickly on a phone with a weak signal, and no platform holding your content. You get every file, and a short video on how to change things yourself.

04

Measured, not admired

Calls and form submissions are the score, not how the homepage looks. I report them monthly in one page of plain English, and I'd rather be judged on that.

Straight answers

The questions people ask before they call.

All questions →
Who owns the website when we're done?

You do. The domain is registered in your name, the site and all its content are yours, and if you ever want to move it elsewhere I'll hand over everything and help the next person get set up.

How long until I see results?

The site goes live in two to three weeks. Local search results usually move in six to twelve weeks; that's how long Google takes, and anyone promising faster is guessing. If we run ads, you'll see traffic within days, though tuning takes a few weeks.

What do you need from me?

About three hours in total: the first call, a session where I ask about your customers, and one round of feedback on the draft. I write the copy, so you don't have to. If you have photos of real jobs, send them; they beat stock photography every time.

How does your pricing compare with an agency?

An agency's quote carries an office, salespeople and account managers. Mine doesn't. What you're paying for is the work itself, done by the person you spoke to, and the fact that I'm still answering emails next spring.
